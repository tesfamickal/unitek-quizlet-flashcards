## 1. Big picture: what this chapter is really about

The nervous system is the body’s **rapid communication and control system**.

It does three big jobs:

1. **Receives information** from inside/outside the body.
2. **Processes and interprets** that information.
3. **Sends commands** that control movement, organ function, reflexes, and behavior.

Mental health is included because mood, consciousness, perception, sleep, behavior, memory, and thought all depend on nervous-system function.

### One-sentence mental model

> **CNS decides, PNS delivers, neurons transmit, neurotransmitters signal, and disorders happen when structure, blood flow, chemicals, insulation, or perception is disrupted.**

---

## 2. CNS vs PNS: memorize this first

| System | Includes | Main job | Quiz trap |
|---|---|---|---|
| **CNS — central nervous system** | Brain + spinal cord | Receives, processes, stores, interprets, coordinates | CNS is **not** cranial/spinal nerves. |
| **PNS — peripheral nervous system** | Cranial nerves + spinal nerves | Carries messages to/from CNS | PNS transmits signals **to and from** CNS. |
| **Autonomic nervous system** | Part of PNS | Controls involuntary organs/glands | Heart rate, digestion, respiratory rate, blood pressure. |
| **Somatic nervous system** | Part of PNS | Voluntary skeletal muscle movement | Think “I choose to move.” |

### Fast recall

- **CNS = brain + spinal cord**.
- **PNS = nerves outside CNS**.
- **Autonomic = automatic organs**.
- **Somatic = voluntary body movement**.

## 3. Structures of the nervous system

| Structure | What it is | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| **Brain** | Main control center | Thought, memory, movement, emotion, senses, vital regulation. |
| **Spinal cord** | Long protected nerve tissue from brainstem down spine | Main pathway between brain and body; also reflex activity. |
| **Nerves** | Bundles of nerve fibers | Carry sensory and motor impulses. |
| **Sensory organs/receptors** | Eyes, ears, nose, skin, tongue receptors | Receive external/internal stimulation. |
| **Neurons** | Basic nerve cells | Transmit impulses. |
| **Glial cells** | Support/protect neurons | Hold neurons in place, supply nutrients/oxygen, insulate, remove debris. |
| **Meninges** | Protective membranes around CNS | Inflammation = meningitis. |
| **CSF** | Cerebrospinal fluid | Cushions, cools, nourishes brain/spinal cord. |

## 4. Neuron anatomy and signal flow

A neuron is the basic cell of the nervous system.

### Signal direction

```text
Dendrites receive → cell body processes → axon carries → axon terminal releases neurotransmitter → synapse crosses → target receptor responds
```

| Part | Function |
|---|---|
| **Dendrites** | Receive messages toward the neuron. |
| **Axon** | Carries impulse away from the neuron cell body. |
| **Axon terminal** | Releases neurotransmitter into synapse. |
| **Synapse** | Gap between neurons/target cells where chemical signaling occurs. |
| **Myelin sheath** | Protective insulation that speeds impulse transmission. |

### Why myelin matters

Demyelination means loss/damage of myelin. This slows or disrupts nerve transmission and is a key idea in **multiple sclerosis** and some neuropathies.

| Neurotransmitter | High-yield association |
|---|---|
| **Acetylcholine** | Nerve impulses at synapses and neuromuscular junctions; muscle action. |
| **Dopamine** | Motivation, pleasure, movement; abnormal levels connect to schizophrenia, Parkinson disease, and movement disorders. |

## 6. Reflexes and receptors

A **reflex** is an automatic, involuntary response to a stimulus.

Examples include:

- changes in heart rate
- changes in respiratory rate
- blood pressure changes
- coughing/sneezing
- withdrawal from painful stimulus

**Receptors** receive stimulation such as pain, touch, pressure, temperature, sound, smell, taste, and visual input. The nervous system interprets that stimulation and sends a response.

## 7. Meninges and cerebrospinal fluid

The brain and spinal cord are protected by three meninges.

| Layer | Description |
|---|---|
| **Dura mater** | Tough outer layer. |
| **Arachnoid mater** | Web-like middle layer. |
| **Pia mater** | Thin delicate inner layer close to brain/spinal cord. |

### CSF — cerebrospinal fluid

CSF is clear, colorless fluid that:

- cushions the brain and spinal cord
- cools/protects CNS tissue
- transports nutrients and chemical messengers

### Disorders linked to this section

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Meningitis** | Inflammation of meninges. |
| **Hydrocephalus** | Excess CSF in brain ventricles. |
| **Meningocele** | Congenital herniation of meninges through skull/spinal column defect. |
| **Encephalocele** | Congenital herniation involving brain tissue. |

## 8. Brain regions: what each part does

| Brain part | Function |
|---|---|
| **Cerebrum** | Largest part; thought, memory, judgment, emotion, sensory/motor integration. |
| **Cerebellum** | Second-largest part; coordination, equilibrium, posture, smooth movement. |
| **Brainstem** | Connects brain and spinal cord; vital functions/reflexes. |
| **Thalamus** | Relay station for sensory impulses to/from cerebrum. |
| **Hypothalamus** | Autonomic/endocrine regulation; temperature, hunger, thirst, sleep-wake cycle, emotion. |
| **Medulla oblongata** | Part of brainstem; controls basic survival functions such as respiration/heart rate reflexes. |
| **Pons** | Brainstem bridge/pathway for impulses. |

### Hypothalamus high-yield list

The hypothalamus helps regulate:

- autonomic nervous system
- heart rate / blood pressure / respiratory rate / digestive activity
- emotional response
- body temperature
- food and water intake
- sleep-wakefulness cycle
- endocrine system through pituitary connection

## 10. Cerebral hemispheres: the crossing rule

The cerebrum has right and left hemispheres.

**High-yield trap:** control usually crosses sides.

| Brain injury side | Body effect |
|---|---|
| Right hemisphere injury | Left-side sensory/motor deficits. |
| Left hemisphere injury | Right-side sensory/motor deficits. |

If a quiz says “right hemisphere controls right side,” that is **false** for most motor/sensory functions.

## 12. Sympathetic vs parasympathetic

| Division | Main idea | Body state |
|---|---|---|
| **Sympathetic** | Fight-or-flight | Stress, emergency, increased alertness. |
| **Parasympathetic** | Rest-and-digest | Returns body toward normal/resting state. |

The chapter notes that parasympathetic nerves help the body return to normal after stress and control many ordinary functions when not physically/emotionally stressed.

## 14. Neurodegenerative diseases

Neurodegenerative diseases involve progressive deterioration of neurons or nervous-system function.

| Disease | Key idea |
|---|---|
| **Alzheimer’s disease** | Progressive decline in memory, thinking, reasoning, judgment; personality changes may occur. |
| **Huntington’s disease** | Genetic/hereditary degenerative disorder. |
| **Parkinson’s disease** | Movement disorder with tremor, rigidity, shuffling gait, reduced control; dopamine is important. |
| **ALS / Lou Gehrig disease** | Progressive deterioration of nerve cells controlling voluntary muscles; weakness/paralysis. |
| **Lewy body dementia** | Dementia associated with abnormal protein deposits. |

### Differentiate dementia vs delirium

| Term | Pattern |
|---|---|
| **Dementia** | Slow progressive decline in mental abilities. |
| **Delirium** | Acute confusion, agitation, hallucination; usually more sudden. |

## 17. Brain tumors and intracranial pressure

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Brain tumor** | Abnormal growth located inside skull. |
| **Glioblastoma** | Aggressive malignant tumor from glial cells. |
| **Benign tumor** | Noncancerous but can still damage brain by pressure. |
| **Malignant tumor** | Cancerous/invasive and destructive. |
| **Intracranial pressure / ICP** | Pressure inside skull; can rise from tumor, bleeding, edema, trauma, or CSF obstruction. |

### Why benign brain tumors are still dangerous

Even if a tumor is not cancerous, the skull is a closed space. Growth can compress brain tissue and cause neurological symptoms.

## 18. Stroke section: CVA, TIA, FAST

A **stroke / CVA / cerebrovascular accident** happens when blood flow to part of the brain is disrupted.

### FAST mnemonic

| Letter | Stands for | What to look for |
|---|---|---|
| F | Facial drooping | One side of face droops. |
| A | Arm drift | One arm drifts downward. |
| S | Speech abnormality | Slurred/strange/unable to speak. |
| T | Time | Time is critical; call emergency help. |

### Types of stroke

| Type | Cause | Key wording |
|---|---|---|
| **Ischemic stroke** | Blockage/narrowing/clot reduces blood flow | Think “blocked blood supply.” |
| **Hemorrhagic stroke** | Blood vessel leaks/ruptures/aneurysm bleeds | Think “bleeding in brain.” |
| **TIA** | Temporary/ministroke symptoms | Warning sign; symptoms may pass but still serious. |

### Quiz trap

If the question says ischemic stroke is caused by blockage of a **vein**, be careful. Course wording marked that false; ischemic stroke is classically arterial blood-flow blockage/clot.

## 20. Spinal cord disorders

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Myelitis** | Inflammation of spinal cord. |
| **Acute flaccid myelitis** | Serious condition often affecting children after viral infection; weakness and reduced reflexes. |
| **Poliomyelitis / polio** | Viral infection affecting gray matter of spinal cord; paralysis risk. |
| **Post-polio syndrome** | Later recurrence/worsening after prior polio. |
| **Myelopathy** | Disease/pathology of spinal cord. |
| **Multiple sclerosis / MS** | Autoimmune inflammatory demyelinating disorder; worsening episodes = flares/exacerbations; periods of improvement = remissions. |

### MS high-yield terms

- **Demyelination** = loss of protective myelin sheath.
- **Exacerbation/flare** = worsening episode.
- **Remission** = symptoms ease, but disease not cured.

## 22. Diagnostic procedures

| Procedure | What it checks |
|---|---|
| **MRI** | Detailed magnetic imaging of brain/spinal structures. |
| **MRA** | Magnetic resonance angiography; blood vessels. |
| **Functional MRI** | Brain blood-flow/activity changes during tasks. |
| **Carotid ultrasonography** | Ultrasound of carotid arteries; stroke-risk blood-flow assessment. |
| **EEG / electroencephalography** | Electrical brain activity; seizures and abnormal brain waves. |
| **Myelography / myelogram** | Spinal canal imaging using contrast. |
| **Electroneurography** | Measures nerve conduction/speed through peripheral nerves. |
| **Lumbar puncture / LP / spinal tap** | CSF sample from subarachnoid space; infection, bleeding, disease clues. |

## 23. Treatments, anesthesia, and procedures

| Treatment/procedure | Key idea |
|---|---|
| **Sedative** | Depresses CNS to calm without necessarily causing sleep. |
| **Phenobarbital** | Barbiturate used as sedative and anticonvulsant. |
| **Anticonvulsant** | Prevents/treats seizures. |
| **Local anesthesia** | Numbs small area. |
| **Regional anesthesia** | Blocks nerve conduction to larger region. |
| **Spinal anesthesia** | Injected into subarachnoid space; lower-body numbness. |
| **Epidural anesthesia** | Injected into epidural space; common in childbirth. |
| **Topical anesthesia** | Applied to surface. |
| **General anesthesia** | Loss of sensation/consciousness for surgery. |
| **Conscious sedation** | Short-acting sedation; patient can often respond but is relaxed/sleepy. |
| **ECT** | Electroconvulsive therapy; controlled seizure for severe psychiatric symptoms. |
| **Lobectomy** | Surgical removal of part of brain lobe. |
| **Thalamotomy** | Surgical lesion in thalamus; tremor control in selected cases. |
| **TMS** | Transcranial magnetic stimulation; noninvasive magnetic pulses for depression/other conditions. |
| **Radiofrequency ablation** | Destroys pain-transmitting nerve tissue with heat. |
| **Neurotomy** | Cutting/dissection of nerve. |
