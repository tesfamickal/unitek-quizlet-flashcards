# 3. Lymphatic system: fluid movement and filtration

## What the lymphatic system does

The chapter’s early pages explain that the lymphatic system has three major jobs:

1. Absorbs fat-soluble vitamins and fats from the small intestine.
2. Removes waste products and excess tissue fluid.
3. Returns fluid back to the bloodstream.

The lymphatic system also works closely with immunity because lymphatic tissues contain immune cells that identify and attack pathogens.

## Interstitial fluid → lymph

Key flow:

```text
blood plasma leaves capillaries
→ becomes interstitial fluid between cells
→ enters lymphatic capillaries
→ is now called lymph
→ travels through lymphatic vessels
→ passes through lymph nodes
→ returns to the bloodstream near the neck/chest
```

Important distinction:

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Interstitial fluid | Fluid between cells/tissues before it enters lymphatic vessels. |
| Lymph | Fluid once it enters lymphatic vessels. |
| Lymphatic capillaries | Tiny blind-ended vessels that pick up tissue fluid. |
| Lymphatic vessels | Larger vessels that carry lymph toward ducts and blood circulation. |

Quiz connections:
- Q10: Lymph is transported in lymphatic vessels.
- Q13: Interstitial fluid comes from plasma that moved out of blood vessels into tissue spaces.
- Q6: The lymphatic system drains lymph from tissues into the blood.

# 4. Lymphatic organs and structures

## Lymph nodes

Lymph nodes are small filtering structures along lymph vessels. They contain lymphocytes and other immune cells.

Study points:
- They filter lymph.
- They help destroy pathogens.
- They can swell when fighting infection.
- Main groups tested: cervical, axillary, inguinal.

Quiz connections:
- Q20: A lymph node contains lymphocytes capable of destroying pathogens.
- Q27: Lymphadenopathy means disease affecting lymph nodes.
- Q74: Major lymph node groups include cervical, axillary, and inguinal.

Word parts:
- `lymphaden/o` = lymph node
- `lymphocyt/o` = lymphocyte
- `lymph/o` = lymph
- `lymphangi/o` = lymphatic vessel

## Tonsils

The tonsils are masses of lymphatic tissue in the throat region. Their job is to guard entrances where pathogens can enter.

Tonsil groups shown in the chapter:
- adenoids / pharyngeal tonsils
- palatine tonsils
- lingual tonsils

Quiz connection:
- Q23: Tonsils help prevent pathogens from entering the respiratory system.

Word parts:
- `tonsill/o` = tonsils
- `adenoid/o` = adenoid

## Thymus

The thymus is a lymphoid tissue near/above the heart. It is important for T-cell maturation, especially earlier in life.

Quiz connection:
- Q28 matching: `thym/o` = thymus.

## Spleen

The spleen is in the left upper quadrant of the abdomen. It filters blood and helps remove foreign material and old blood cells.

High-yield spleen facts:
- The spleen filters microorganisms and foreign material from the blood.
- A ruptured spleen is a medical emergency.
- The spleen is not in the right upper quadrant; it is on the left side.
- Splenomegaly means enlarged spleen.
- Splenorrhagia means bleeding from the spleen.

Quiz connections:
- Q39: Splenomegaly = abnormal enlargement of the spleen.
- Q55: The spleen is not in the right upper quadrant.
- Q60: Ruptured spleen = medical emergency.
- Q61: Spleen filters microorganisms and foreign materials from blood.
- Q78: Splenorrhagia = bleeding from the spleen.

Word parts:
- `splen/o` = spleen
- `-megaly` = enlargement
- `-rrhagia` = bleeding

## Appendix

The appendix hangs from the lower portion of the cecum and may have an immune role.

Quiz connection:
- Q34: The appendix hangs from the lower portion of the cecum.

# 5. Immune system basics

## First line of defense

The chapter describes the body’s early defenses as barriers and immediate protective systems.

High-yield examples:
- intact skin
- mucous membranes
- respiratory tract filtering
- acidic/chemical defenses
- lymphatic filtration

Quiz connections:

## Antigens and antibodies

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Antigen (Ag) | Any substance the body sees as foreign. |
| Antibody (Ab) | Protein made by immune cells to respond to a specific antigen. |
| Antigen-antibody reaction | Immune recognition/response between antigen and antibody. |

| Cell/type | Basic job |
|---|---|

| T cells | Help coordinate immune responses and attack infected/abnormal cells. |

# 6. Types of immunity

This is a big quiz area. Learn the differences by asking: “Did the body make the response itself, or did it receive protection from somewhere else?”

## Passive immunity

Passive immunity means protection is received from another source. The body does not have to make the antibodies itself.

Examples:
- antibodies passed through breast milk
- some antibody therapies / immune globulin products

Quiz connection:
- Q4: Breastfeeding passes passive immunity through breast milk.

## Active / acquired immunity

Active immunity means the body builds its own immune response after exposure.

Examples:
- having had a contagious disease
- vaccination, because the body responds to antigen exposure and builds memory

Quiz connections:
- Q15: Acquired immunity can be obtained after having a contagious disease.
- Q56: COVID vaccines were meant to create active/acquired immunity; exact Canvas wording needs checking because `active` was not accepted in that attempt.

## Natural vs acquired wording trap

The quiz tested a wording trap:

- “Resistance after exposure to disease or administration of antigen” sounds like acquired/active immunity, not natural immunity.

Quiz connection:
- Q2: The statement about natural immunity was false.

# 7. Allergies and altered immune responses

## Allergy

An allergy is an overreaction to a substance that triggers the immune system. The trigger is an allergen, not an antibody.

Quiz connections:
- Q44: Allergy is not an overreaction to a particular antibody; it is a response to an allergen/antigen.
- Q73: Allergen = substance that produces allergic reaction.

## Anaphylaxis

Anaphylaxis is a severe systemic allergic reaction. Bee stings are a classic example.

Quiz connection:
- Q64: Bee sting systemic reaction = anaphylaxis.

## Scratch test

A scratch test is a diagnostic skin test used to identify common allergies.

Quiz connection:
- Q76: Diagnostic skin test for allergies = scratch test.

## Antihistamines

Antihistamines relieve or prevent symptoms of certain allergies.

Quiz connection:
- Q7: Allergy symptom medications = antihistamines.

## Specialists

| Specialist | What they do |
|---|---|
| Allergist | Diagnoses/treats allergies and altered immune responses. |
| Immunologist | Diagnoses/treats immune system disorders. |
| Hematologist | Blood disorders. |
| Oncologist | Malignant disorders/cancer. |
| Lymphologist | Lymphatic system disorders. |

Quiz connections:

- Q32: Correct spelling: allergist.
- Q47: Immunologist does not simply mean lymphatic-system specialist.

# 8. Autoimmune disorders and immunodeficiency

## Autoimmune disorders

Autoimmune disorders happen when the immune system attacks the body’s own tissues.

Quiz connection:
- Q31: Autoimmune disorder = antibodies against own tissues.

Examples shown in the chapter’s table included autoimmune conditions affecting different systems, such as joint/connective tissue, muscle, nervous system, digestive system, endocrine system, and others.

## Immunodeficiency

Immunodeficiency means the immune system is weakened or missing parts of normal defense.

Examples discussed:
- severe combined immunodeficiency
- HIV / AIDS

## HIV and AIDS

HIV stands for Human Immunodeficiency Virus. HIV can damage immune defenses and may progress to AIDS. AIDS is associated with opportunistic infections and cancers such as Kaposi’s sarcoma.

Quiz connections:
- Q14: AIDS and Kaposi’s sarcoma are complications associated with HIV.
- Q35: HIV = Human Immunodeficiency Virus.

# 9. Pathogens and infections

A pathogen is a disease-causing organism. The quiz expects you to separate virus, bacteria, fungus, and parasite.

## Viruses

Virus-related conditions in this chapter/quiz:

| Disease/term | Study point |
|---|---|
| Influenza | Viral respiratory infection; commonly called the flu. |
| Herpes zoster | Shingles; viral, not bacterial. |
| Varicella zoster | Chickenpox virus; related to shingles. |
| Rabies | Viral infection transmitted through bite/saliva of infected animals. |
| Mumps | Viral infection causing swelling of parotid glands. |
| Ebola | Rare/fatal viral disease, not parasite. |
| HIV | Human Immunodeficiency Virus. |
| Rubella | Part of MMR vaccine; separate from shingles. |

Quiz connections:
- Q3: Ebola is not transmitted by a parasite; it is viral.
- Q12: Mumps causes parotid gland swelling.
- Q18: Correct spelling: influenza.
- Q19: Rabies through bite/saliva of infected animals.
- Q40: Shingles = herpes zoster.
- Q51: Herpes zoster is not bacterial.
- Q62: Correct spelling: varicella.
- Q63: Influenza is not bacterial; it is viral.
- Q75: Infectious mononucleosis is not shingles.

## Bacteria and antibiotics

Antibiotics kill or inhibit bacterial growth. Bactericides cause bacterial death.

Important antibiotic-resistant bacteria mentioned in the chapter:
- MRSA: methicillin-resistant Staphylococcus aureus.
- Some resistant bacteria survive drug treatment and spread resistance.

Quiz connections:
- Q45: Bactericide = medication causing death of bacteria.
- Q65: Antibiotics kill/inhibit bacteria.
- Q69: Antibiotic-resistant bacteria occur when surviving bacteria become resistant to a drug.

## Fungi

Fungal infections include conditions such as athlete’s foot and candidiasis/yeast-related infections.

Quiz connections:
- Q1: Yeast infections do not occur in cold, dry areas; they prefer warm/moist areas.
- Q22: Correct spelling: antifungals.
- Q67: Athlete’s foot is caused by a fungus.

## Parasites

Parasites live on or within another organism at the expense of the host.

Examples:
- malaria is caused by a parasite
- toxoplasmosis can be transmitted from contaminated animal feces

Quiz connections:
- Q9: Toxoplasmosis is commonly transmitted from pets to humans through contaminated animal feces.
- Q16: Correct spelling: parasites.
- Q46: Malaria’s etiology is a parasite.

# 12. Cancer and oncology

## Basic cancer vocabulary

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Tumor | Abnormal growth/mass. |
| Benign | Noncancerous; usually not life-threatening. |
| Malignant | Cancerous/life-threatening. |
| Metastasis | Spread of cancer to a new site. |
| Metastasize | Verb: to spread. |
| Oncology | Study/prevention/causes/treatment of tumors and cancer. |
| Oncologist | Specialist who treats cancer/malignant disorders. |

Quiz connections:
- Q29: Benign tumors are not life-threatening.
- Q36: Malignant = life-threatening.
- Q53: Breast cancer may metastasize to lymph nodes and other sites.
- Q77: Oncology = study of tumors/cancer.

## Metastasis spelling trap

Correct word:

```text
metastasis
```

Do not write:
- matastasis
- mestatasis

Quiz connection:
- Q33: Correct spelling is `metastasis`.

## Staging tumors

The chapter shows staging as a way to describe how far cancer has progressed. Higher stage generally means more spread/involvement.

Core idea:

```text
localized tumor
→ invasion near original site
→ lymph node involvement
→ distant spread/metastasis
```

## Cancer treatments

Common treatment types shown in the chapter:

| Treatment | Simple meaning |
|---|---|
| Surgery | Removes tumor/tissue. |
| Lumpectomy | Removes a breast lump/tumor and nearby tissue. |
| Mastectomy | Removes breast tissue. |
| Chemotherapy | Uses chemical agents/drugs against cancer cells. |
| Radiation therapy | Uses radiation to destroy cancer tissue while trying to spare healthy tissue. |
| Brachytherapy | Radiation placed close to/inside tumor site. |
| Teletherapy | External radiation from outside the body. |
| Tomotherapy | CT-guided radiation approach. |
| Immunotherapy | Uses immune response to fight disease/cancer. |

Quiz connection:
- Q30: Lumpectomy and mastectomy are surgical treatments for breast cancer.
